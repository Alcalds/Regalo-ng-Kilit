About Regalo ng Kilit

Conservation that gives back to people and place.

RnK connects biodiversity conservation with regenerative agriculture, education, food security, sustainable livelihoods, and community action across the Calamianes.

Why Regalo ng Kilit?

A gift from the Kilit to the community.

The foundation's story grew from the belief that protecting wildlife cannot be separated from the lives of people who share the same landscape. The Kilit became a symbol for a conservation approach that gives something back through education, food production, community development, and livelihood opportunity.

“It is about making the Kilit a lasting gift to the people, the landscape, and the future of Palawan.”
Founder's Perspective

Stewardship as responsibility.

For Dr. Manuel R. Reyes, BERAE is more than a technical framework. He describes it as a personal calling rooted in Christian faith, where biodiversity enhancement, regenerative agriculture, and sustainable enterprise are interdependent responsibilities expressed through stewardship, service, and community empowerment.

This reflection presents Dr. Reyes' personal perspective on BERAE and is distinct from RnK's formal Vision and Mission below.

Our Vision

Healthy biodiversity, healthy soil, healthy food, healthy income, and healthy community for a healthy you.

Our Mission

To enhance biodiversity and regenerate soil while profitably and equitably growing and distributing nutritious food and boosting tourism.

Our Journey

Selected milestones from the RnK story.

RnK’s work began before its formal establishment in 2019, with community and school initiatives that later expanded into conservation, education, regenerative agriculture, scholarships, and community development.

2018

The work begins

Early initiatives included a water facility, sports complex, scholarship support, and the first Kilit Festival.

2019

Formal establishment

RnK was formally established in 2019, alongside continuing Kilit Festival activities.

2020

Gardens + biodiversity

School-plus-home garden and biodiversity enhancement training expands the connection between food production and conservation.

2021

Regenerative agriculture

Capacity building for Coron Natural Farms and support for small enterprise expanded RnK's regenerative agriculture work.

2022

Education + community

School facilities, scholarships, Kilit Festival activities, and an international SHGBEE conference broadened the foundation's education and community work.

2023–2024

Growing the network

Scholarships, student support, festivals, SHGBEE activities, community programs, and additional conservation-related initiatives continue.

How We Work

Community-led. Science-informed. Locally rooted.
